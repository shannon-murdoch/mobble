## The narrative in prose

Mobble began with a father and son on a sheep and cattle farm in Victoria, trying to put the farm's records in one place they could both rely on. Eight years later more than 4,000 farmers use it. The problem it solves hasn't changed much, but the pressure behind it has grown.

### Problem

Livestock farmers want to run a profitable business and keep the right to sell, with everyone who works the place knowing:

- **which mobs are where**
- **what's been done to them**
- **what comes next**

Three gaps stand in the way:

- **Records** are made in the paddock by several people but needed in the office by one.
- The **knowledge** to work the property safely sits in one or two heads.
- The **obligations** keep growing while labour, signal and time stay short:
  - LPA reaccreditation every two years
  - a biosecurity plan that must be uploaded or created at accreditation, since 1 September 2026
  - eID for every sheep and farmed goat leaving a property, from 1 January 2027 in NSW, Queensland, SA, Tasmania and the ACT

The sharpest painkiller is proving compliance when someone asks. Next come knowing exact mob numbers and status, and making **withholding periods visible** to whoever moves or sells stock.

Grazing optimisation, sustainability evidence and succession records are real, but they're slower 'vitamins'.

### Audience

Fit shows most clearly with owner-operated southern Australian sheep and mixed sheep-and-cattle family farms (roughly 800 to 24,000 sheep equivalent, LPA-accredited, with two to ten people recording).

It also shows with multi-property family businesses that employ permanent staff, New Zealand hill-country stations under NZFAP, and mixed farms already on Agworld. On those farms the users are **family**, **permanent staff** and the **office** partner.

Outside the farm they're the accountant, the auditor and the agronomist.

Compliance pain shows up in every state and in New Zealand. The state decides which obligation comes next. Grazing tools matter most to intensive rotational grazers. Beef cow-calf families lead the Future list because cattle already account for about 55% of Mobble's stocking units. Northern stations come later: the map-and-water job fits them, but Mobble has no customers there yet.

### Value proposition

*One simple farm record your whole team keeps. Audit-ready every day.* The two halves depend on each other. A record the whole team keeps every day is what makes the audit-day promise true. Five sub-benefits follow:

1. Audit-ready every day.
2. Everyone on the same page, without the phone calls.
3. Know what you've got and where it is.
4. Tax time without the spreadsheet chase.
5. Works where your phone signal doesn't.

### Competitive advantage

In the short term Mobble wins by being:

- **mob-first and fluent in sheep**
- **simple** enough for the whole family
- **usable offline**
- **priced competitively** per head against the few rivals that publish comparable prices

Free data import and **support** that customers name personally help too.

Its one durable power today is switching costs: years of paddock and mob history, team habits and partner logins.

The power worth building is network economies through Connect, because each accountant, agent, adviser and auditor serves many farms.

Counter-positioning against AgriWebb, as it moves to URUS ownership, is tempting. It stays narrow until switchers show up.

### Growth

Farmer-to-farmer referral, field days and partner co-marketing drive growth today. The record shows its shape:

- users roughly doubled in 2022
- since then Mobble has added a near-constant 400 to 600 users a year
- so annual growth has slid from about 100% to about 13% to 14% in 2025 and 2026

Constant additions like these are what an engine run on people, events and word of mouth produces. That's steady but slow for a business that wants to "continue to scale across AU/NZ and beyond".

The next stage should run through people who touch many farms. In the short term that means three hand-run channels:

- offering Team on the call Mobble already makes before each renewal
- sessions for **stock agents** and **rural accountants**
- clinics and webinars timed to the biosecurity-plan and eID deadlines

In the long term it means turning those channels into loops with a multi-farm partner view, invite attribution and business referrals.

### Business model

Mobble sells an annual subscription priced by livestock capacity on two plans, with Enterprise rates above the top band. We estimate roughly A$1.1M to A$2.7M in annual recurring revenue. For a one-user farm of up to 4,000 sheep, list prices have risen about 76% since 2020 while users kept growing, which suggests real pricing power.

The levers, in order:

1. **Retention** is the biggest lever.
2. **Cheaper acquisition** through Connect members comes next.
3. **The Core-to-Team upgrade** is third, and it matters more for retention than for revenue.

### What could break it

Five assumptions carry the most weight, so they're tested first:

1. Staying mob-based costs few customers once every sheep needs eID.
2. Audits really are the purchase trigger.
3. Farms will pay the Team premium.
4. Connect members can bring in new farms.
5. Northern stations and North American ranchers want the same job done.

The cohesion check (section 7) finds that some of today's pricing and referral settings work against the very moat and loops this narrative relies on.

## 1. Problem to Solve

### 1.1 Initial insight

Mobble started on the Lawrence family's fourth-generation sheep and cattle farm at Avenel, Victoria. When Jock Lawrence came back to the farm, he and his father tried to put all its records in one place. "We really struggled to do that in a nice, efficient way," he told LaunchVic.

The software they tried "was either too clunky, over-complicated or angled towards other types of farming". He wanted to end "the double handling of information" and the "pocket notebook which is often lost, dropped in a drain or destroyed in the wash", as The Farmer magazine put it. By October 2018 nine farming businesses were testing the software, and Mobble kicked off that November.

Read as problem statements, the origin story gives three hypotheses:

1. **Records were split** across notebook, spreadsheet and memory, and handled twice. The pain was consolidation and trust.
2. **More than one person made the records** (father and son), so the need was one shared version everyone could rely on.
3. **The tools on offer were built for a different farm** (intensive, cropping or individual-animal), so a mob-based family farm paid a complexity cost for depth it didn't use.

Why it matters: the founding problem is shared, trustworthy records for a family team on a mob-based livestock farm. That's a wider job than digital record keeping, and it points straight at the team and compliance jobs the new Core and Team plans price around.

### 1.2 Initial hypothesis

The **outcome** the owner or manager of a family livestock business wants is to run the place profitably and keep the right to sell, while everyone who works on it knows which mobs are where, what's been done to them and what needs doing next, without the owner having to be there to say so.

Their **motivation** is mostly economic and defensive. Income depends on selling stock into markets that require proof: LPA accreditation and National Vendor Declarations (NVDs) in Australia, NZFAP in New Zealand, and processor and export audits on top. Biosecurity sits at the top of farmers' own concerns. Behind that come staff safety, time with family, and a business the next generation can take over.

Three **gaps** stand in the way.

- **Records are made in the paddock by several people but needed in the office by one.** They're written in notebooks, sent as texts or remembered, then typed up late or not at all. When an auditor, buyer, agent or accountant asks for proof, the farm spends days assembling it, and sometimes can't.
- **The knowledge to work the property safely sits in one or two heads.** Paddock names, waters, hazards, which mob is on a withholding period: every new staff member, contractor or family helper depends on the owner for it, so the owner becomes the bottleneck.
- **Obligations keep growing while labour, signal and time don't.** LPA reaccreditation runs every two years ($90 plus GST per two-year period since 1 September 2024), and from 1 September 2026 it requires a biosecurity plan to be uploaded or created. All sheep and farmed goats need eID from 1 January 2027 in NSW, Queensland, SA, Tasmania and the ACT. EUCAS beef needs an EU geolocation before the EUDR applies on 30 December 2026. Meanwhile 87% of farmers say red tape is increasing.

The NSW eID date may move: NSW Farmers members voted in July 2026 to ask for January 2029, though no change had been announced by 30 October 2026.

### 1.3 The three-step process, by role

#### Owner or manager (decision maker)

**Outcome.** They want to:

- sell stock when and where they choose, without paperwork blocking the sale
- know at any moment how many stock they have, where, in what condition and with what treatment status
- get the right jobs done on time by other people, without explaining everything themselves
- lift production per hectare over time
- hand on a business, and a history of it, that someone else can run

"We were having trouble knowing exactly what stock we had, where and how many at any point in time," says Andrew Paterson of Matakanui Station, Central Otago.

**Motivation.**

| Motivation | Why it matters to an owner | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Market access** | Without LPA accreditation there are no NVDs or eNVDs, and the farm "may have issues with selling or moving your livestock at saleyards or processing plants" | High |
| **Audits** | ISC audits 2,500 producers at random each year plus 1,000 targeted; unclosed corrective actions can suspend accreditation | High |
| **Biosecurity** | First in the NFF's 2024 survey, 94% concerned (n=1,026). A large multi-state FMD outbreak is estimated at "around $80 billion" over 10 years | High |
| **Staff safety and retention** | 53 deaths and 182 serious injuries on Australian farms in 2025; 80.5% of farmers concerned about worker shortages | Medium to High |
| **Profit** | Unrecorded deaths, empty troughs and overgrazing cut margin; on Paterson's station unrecorded casualties hid a disease problem | Medium (not quantified) |
| **Animal welfare** | One of LPA's seven standards; a water failure is a welfare event | Medium |
| **Time with family** | Farmers need to be "in the paddock, not behind a desk", one producer told Beef Central | Medium |
| **Succession** | Mobble's own story is fourth- and seventh-generation, but nothing links records to succession directly | Low to Medium |
| **Buyer and sustainability evidence** | Without a property geolocation, EUCAS beef "may not be eligible for export to the EU" | Low for most farms, High for the EUCAS niche |

**Gaps.**

- **A1. Many hands record where there's no signal, and one person consolidates late.** Telstra's "claimed coverage is still only 35%" of Australia's area, and 77% of NSW Farmers survey respondents lose signal away from major buildings. Use also seems to lapse over summer, judging by Mobble's February webinar for users "getting back into" their records.
- **A2. Assembling proof takes longer than the work it proves.** Family farmers told Beef Central about printing "hundreds and hundreds of pages of records for every audit". One lotfeeder: "Our audit used to take 2-3 hours and now it takes 5-6 hours and it is currently $255 an hour, plus GST."
- **A3. The owner is the single point of knowledge.** Pipelines and other infrastructure live in people's heads, and staff phone to ask.
- **A4. The rules change faster than farm routines.** The biosecurity plan, eID and EUDR steps all fall in 2026–27. Josie Angus, a Central Queensland producer and processor, recalls LPA's original pitch: "all you will need is your Coopers notebook".
- **A5. Stock counts drift and nobody can say why.** Mobble added an "other" reconciliation category because numbers go missing.
- **A6. Labour is thin, turns over and is often family.** Broadacre farms "make greater use of family members and local workers".
- **A7. The tools on offer fit somebody else's farm:** too clunky, built for intensive farming, or crop-dominant.

Why it matters: A1 and A2 are the owner's sharpest pains, which keeps offline field capture and audit-ready output at the centre of the product. A3 and A6 are the case for charging for team access, which the Team plan now does.

#### Family member or bookkeeper

**Outcome.** Keep the books, compliance paperwork and family communication right with the least friction, know what's happening without being in the paddock, and be able to step in when the main operator is away, sick or retiring. Ruth Kneebone, who runs a family-only operation at Everton, Victoria, showed her auditor the treatment records straight from her phone.

**Motivation.** Tax and cash flow come first: the ATO says "You must value your livestock at the end of each income year". Then peace of mind at audit time, fewer repeated questions inside the family, and being ready to take over.

**Gaps.**

- **B1. Information arrives second-hand and late,** through notebooks, texts and memory.
- **B2. Year-end numbers get rebuilt from scratch.** Opening and closing head by class, natural increase, purchases, sales and deaths all feed the ATO valuation.
- **B3. They can't easily check a record is complete,** because counts drift and manual edits aren't explained.
- **B4. Not every family member is confident with apps.** Outside capital cities the Digital Ability gap is 7.8 points.

Mobble's stories show plenty of family users but no named bookkeeper, so whether the bookkeeper is a distinct role or the owner's partner is still open.

#### Staff, station hands and contractors

**Outcome.** Do the job right the first time, safely, and get home, knowing where to go and what's already been done. A contractor wants to find the job, do it, get paid and leave.

"The map feature has been really useful for new people finding their way around," says John Baker of Boroondarah Pastoral, Middlemount, Queensland. Henry Hindmarsh of Fenland Station, New Zealand: "Our Junior Shepherd found it really helpful when she started."

**Motivation.** Safety, and not being blamed for an avoidable mistake such as moving a mob that's on a withholding period. Purpose, career and pay.

**Gaps.**

- **C1. Directions are verbal, and maps live in heads or on paper.** "Before Mobble, we only had paper maps," says Hindmarsh.
- **C2. They can't see the status they're responsible for:** withholding periods, water levels, what's due.
- **C3. Signal is weak or absent where the work happens.**
- **C4. Inductions are short and turnover is high, worst in the north.** NT station managers called 2018 "the toughest year for recruitment".
- **C5. Contractors are there for days,** and need just enough to find the paddock, see the hazards and log the job.

#### Connect members

Each of these serves many farms, and each one's outcome depends on the quality of the farmer's records.

| Sub-role | Outcome they want | Main gap | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accountant** | A reconciled year-end livestock schedule and valuation for each client | Numbers arrive late as spreadsheets or handwritten summaries that don't reconcile | Medium |
| **Auditor** (LPA, NZFAP, processor) | Verify conformance quickly | Records are scattered or on paper. ANZCO reports a rising volume of NZFAP non-conformances for dog dosing and cattle NAIT records | Medium |
| **Stock agent** | Sell clients' stock on time with accurate numbers, treatment status and documents | The data sits with the farmer, so agents phone around | Low |
| **Farm adviser** | Recommendations that get done | Can't see what was done across clients. "You can't have a grazing course without Mobble," says Susie Bate of Benacre | Low to Medium |
| **Agronomist** | Stock kept off a treated paddock inside its withholding period | Crop and livestock records sit in different systems | Medium |

Why it matters: Connect members feel the farmer's gaps second-hand, so their pull for better records can recruit and retain farmers. Whether that becomes a network effect depends on giving them a reason to use Mobble across clients (see Competitive Advantage).

### 1.4 Vitamin or painkiller: twelve problems ranked

A score of 5 is a painkiller: a severe consequence, a hard trigger, and a legal or market reason to act. A score of 1 is a vitamin: nice to fix, with no forcing moment.

| Rank | Problem | Who feels it | Trigger | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Proving compliance when someone asks** | Owner/manager, bookkeeper, auditor | Two-yearly LPA reaccreditation; random, targeted, processor and NZFAP audits | **5** at the trigger (3 between) |
| 2 | **Not knowing exact numbers, location and status of every mob** | Owner/manager, family, accountant, agent | Sale, muster, EOFY, losses | **4** |
| 3 | **Treatment and withholding status invisible to whoever moves or sells stock** | Owner, staff, agent, agronomist | Dispatch and NVD; grazing after spraying | **4** |
| 4 | **One person holds the farm's operating knowledge** | Owner, staff, family | Hiring, holidays, illness, handover | **4** |
| 5 | **Recording individual sheep and goats on every movement (eID)** | Sheep and goat producers in NSW, Qld, SA, Tas and the ACT; studs; agents | Legacy exemption ends 1 Jan 2027 | **4** (5 in those five jurisdictions through 2027; 3 in Vic and WA) |
| 6 | **Rebuilding year-end livestock numbers for tax** | Owner, bookkeeper, accountant | EOFY (30 June) | **3** (painkiller in June and July) |
| 7 | **Water and infrastructure failures out of sight** | Owner/manager, staff | Heat, drought, pump trips, a new bore runner | **3** (4 for large or northern, 2 for small high-rainfall farms) |
| 8 | **Getting new and short-term people productive and safe on unfamiliar country** | Manager, new staff, contractors | Turnover, muster season, contractor jobs | **3** |
| 9 | **Stock you don't own, or paddocks you share or rent** | Agisters, co-owners | Drought, fire, agistment deals | **3** |
| 10 | **Matching stocking rate to feed and resting paddocks** | Owner/manager, grazing adviser | Dry seasons, autumn break | **2** (4 in drought) |
| 11 | **Evidence for buyers' sustainability and welfare demands** | EUCAS beef, RWS wool, larger enterprises | EUDR from 30 Dec 2026; RWS audits | **2** (4 in the EUCAS niche) |
| 12 | **Keeping the farm's history usable across generations** | Owner, next generation | Succession, sale, illness | **2** |

Any one farm rarely gets a random LPA audit: 2,500 a year over 194,441 accredited producers (the April 2020 count) is 1.29% a year, or about 12% over ten years. But every farm faces the two-yearly self-assessment, and every sale needs a valid NVD.

**Why the ranking falls this way**

In the top three, someone outside the farm asks for proof or a status, the cost of failing falls on the owner, and the paper workaround fails at exactly that moment. Compliance ranks first because no NVDs means no sale, and its triggers now come on a fixed two-year cycle. Withholding status is as severe per event but rarer. Mob numbers matter daily and feed problems 1, 3 and 6. Problem 4 is chronic, and it compounds the others, because records made by other people are the raw material for every kind of proof.

eID has a hard legal date and still ranks fifth. The acute part is a one-off transition, much of the scanning and database work happens at saleyards, agents and processors, and Victorian and WA producers already have routines. Hardware and genetics vendors are racing to own it, and it needs validating more than any other row.

Tax time is a guaranteed annual pain, but the accountant absorbs much of it and nothing stops a sale. Water and onboarding are painkillers only for large, dry, staffed or northern properties, so they matter more to the Future audience (see Target Audience). Problems 9 to 12 are episodic, slow or niche.

Overall, Mobble is a painkiller at compliance, sale and tax triggers and a vitamin in between, so the risk is lapsed use between triggers. Daily capture (problems 2 and 4) has to be worth doing for its own sake, or the painkiller value won't be there when the trigger arrives.

### 1.5 Context

#### State of the market

![Australian flock, herd and farm numbers, 1985–2025](charts/market_40yr.png)

**Forty years of change.** The market Mobble serves has turned from sheep towards cattle and shrunk in farm count:

- **Sheep:** the flock went from 145.6 million in 1985 to a peak of 170.3 million in 1990, then fell to 65.8 million by 2025, down 55% on 1985.
- **Cattle:** the herd grew from 22.8 million to 29.7 million over the same period. Part of that rise is a change in how cattle were counted from 2005, when feedlots were added. Dairy cattle fell from 2.8 million to 2.1 million.
- **Mix:** Australia ran 6.4 sheep per head of cattle in 1985 and 2.2 in 2025.
- **Farms:** counts fell within every count basis:
  - from about 154,000 to 130,000 between 1992 and 2005
  - from about 154,000 to 123,000 between 2006 and 2015
  - steady at 86,000 to 89,000 since 2016, when the cut-off rose to $40,000 of agricultural output

Why it matters: the market has moved towards cattle and towards fewer farms. That shrinks the pool of sheep-only prospects, so cattle features and multi-person farms matter more to Mobble's growth than the founding sheep story suggests.

**Structure: fewer farms, fewer sheep, more beef.** Broadacre and dairy farms fell about 33% to about 56,500 between 1999–2000 and 2022–23, and sheep-beef farms fell 61%. Specialist sheep farms fell in NSW (27%), Vic (33%) and Qld (82%) and rose in SA (12%) and WA (10%). Specialist beef farms rose everywhere except SA (−34%) and the NT (−27%). The flock was 65.8 million at June 2025, down 8.0% in a year, against 27.6 million beef cattle. Why it matters: problems 1 to 4 have to be solved as well for cattle as for sheep, and beef brings demand for weights and individual data.

**Adoption.** In 2016–17, 96% of farmers used phones or computers and over 80% used them for record keeping, but current farm-software adoption hasn't been measured independently as far as we can tell (Mobble's own share of livestock is in Target Audience). In the US, 62.8% of cow-calf producers who described their method kept handwritten records (2022). Why it matters: paper is still the main competitor, so the problem includes getting people to start recording and keep going.

**Connectivity.** Coverage is thin (see A1), and 81.8% of farmers are concerned about access to telecommunications. Any answer to problems 1 to 4 has to work offline.

**Labour.** More of the work falls to people other than the owner (see A6 and C4), which raises problems 4 and 8 and makes team access worth paying for.

**Regulation.** 87% of farmers say red tape is increasing, and 83.5% say government isn't doing enough to reduce it. In the UK, farm assurance costs have some sheep farmers considering dropping out of the Red Tractor scheme. Why it matters: relief from the time cost of proof is a stronger promise than better insight.

**Competitor moves in 2026.**

- **AgriWebb:** URUS, a bovine genetics group, agreed to buy it in May 2026, subject to regulatory approvals; completion hasn't been publicly confirmed.
- **MaiaGrazing** is "closed to new accounts as we focus on Atlas Grazing".
- **Breedr** raised a US$27M Series B on 26 August 2026, partly to expand into Australia and New Zealand.
- **Halter** reported "over 230 beef farms signing up in 2026 alone" in Australia, including the NT.
- **Datamars** launched a platform tied to Tru-Test scales and EID readers on 10 September 2026.
- **FarmIQ** launched a free "Grow" app in New Zealand.

Why it matters: new capital is going into individual-animal, genetics and hardware-linked data (problems 5, 7 and 10). The compliance and team problems (1 to 4) see less new entry, and two incumbents changing hands create switching moments (see Competitive Advantage).

#### Historical attempts

| Approach | What it got right | What it got wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **Pocket notebook** | Cheap, always in the pocket, works without signal | One copy, easily lost, invisible to others, has to be retyped |
| **Paper maps and whiteboards** | Shared at the homestead | Static, stay at the house, go out of date |
| **Spreadsheets** | Flexible, free, accountants like them | Office-bound, one editor at a time, no field capture, version drift |
| **Earlier farm software** | Centralised data and reports | Too clunky, built for intensive or cropping farms, failed under load or without signal |

These attempts assumed one record-keeper in an office, reliable connectivity, an individual-animal or cropping data model, and farmers willing to trade simplicity for depth. Why it matters: "many hands, poor signal, mob-level, minimal effort" should stay as constraints on any solution, which matches the Head of Product brief to protect "the simple, easy-to-use experience our farmers love".

## 2. Target Audience

### 2.1 Initial hypothesis

The most relevant **attributes** are enterprise type, scale (sheep-equivalent capacity, which is also how Mobble prices), the number of people who make records, the region (which sets the compliance regime and the eID timetable), and the operator's preference for simplicity over analytical depth.

We believe owner-operated **southern Australian sheep and mixed sheep-and-cattle family farms**, roughly 800 to 24,000 sheep equivalent, with two to ten people recording and an LPA obligation, feel the problems in Problem to Solve most sharply and get the most from Mobble today. That's where most named customers sit, where the product's language comes from (drench, lamb marking, DSE, wool withhold) and where Mobble's field team works.

Three more segments complete the Now list:

- **Multi-property family businesses with permanent staff** resonate even more strongly, because team coordination is their daily pain and they're the natural buyers of the unlimited-member Team plan.
- **New Zealand sheep and beef hill-country stations** are a proven second geography, with an audit regime (NZFAP) that maps onto the same job.
- **Mixed crop-livestock farms already on Agworld** benefit from a live integration others would need to build.

For **end users**, the Now audience is the family members, office partners and permanent staff of those farms. For **Connect members**, it's the accountant at tax time, the auditor at audit time and the agronomist through Agworld.

Beef cow-calf families, small holders, regenerative grazers, corporates, northern stations, agistment-heavy operators, studs and North American ranches are **Future** segments. Each is gated by a capability, channel or proof point Mobble doesn't have yet. Dairy, goats and other species, and the UK sit outside the plan for now.

### 2.5 State and regional differentiators

Proof of compliance is a top trigger for audited owner-operators in every Australian state and in New Zealand where there's customer evidence. The state sets which obligation comes next, and that's what Mobble's messaging and onboarding should key off.

#### Sheep and goat eID timing

| Jurisdiction | Born on or after | All animals leaving a property | Status at 30 October 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vic | 1 Jan 2017 | 1 Jan 2022 | In force for nearly five years |
| WA | 1 Jan 2025 | 1 Jul 2026 | In force |
| NT | 1 Jan 2025 (all) | 1 Jan 2025 | In force, goats included |
| NSW, Qld, SA, Tas, ACT | 1 Jan 2025 | 1 Jan 2027 | Legacy exemption ends in about two months; NSW Farmers wants NSW moved to January 2029 |

So the next obligation differs:

- **NSW, Qld, SA, Tas and the ACT:** the end of the legacy eID exemption on 1 January 2027.
- **Vic and WA:** already through eID, so next come two-yearly LPA reaccreditation with the biosecurity plan, and processor or RWS audits.
- **New Zealand:** NZFAP, where dog dosing and cattle NAIT records are a growing source of non-conformances (which probably explains Mobble's dog-record "hack" for compliance).

Why it matters: in NSW, Qld, SA and Tas, individual-animal tracking is the live compliance question, and Mobble's help sends those producers to Excel. In Vic and WA eID is an integration question (getting data out of wands, scales and the NLIS database), and hardware vendors such as Datamars are moving on that data. NSW is the biggest sheep state, and its date may move.

#### Northern scale and water

- **NT:** 225 pastoral leases, with beef worth "around $1.2 billion annually". In 2010 the average cattle property was 2,794 km² with 11,029 head, and "staff availability (24%)" was the biggest management hurdle.
- **WA:** 431 pastoral stations on 489 leases over about 856,000 km², with cattle about 55% in the Kimberley, 20% in the Pilbara and 25% further south.
- **Water at scale:** one Barkly aggregation runs 603 water points from 64 bores.
- **Units:** Mobble already supports AE and LSU for northern cattle.

The shared map already helps new staff in central Queensland, NSW and New Zealand. But Mobble has no NT, Kimberley or Pilbara customer (its northernmost are in Central Queensland) and no team member in the NT, while Halter already reports NT customers. Some stations may induct new hands by ride-along with an experienced bore runner, which would weaken the need for a map.

Why it matters: northern stations are a Future segment with no customers yet. They're few in number and large per account (Team or Enterprise pricing), and their needs (water, staff turnover, offline) are the segment-specific painkillers ranked 7 and 8 in Problem to Solve.

#### Connectivity, labour and New Zealand

- **Connectivity.** 64% of NSW Farmers respondents are unsatisfied with mobile coverage and 63% report dropouts. Offline is table stakes everywhere, and decisive in the north and on large southern properties.
- **Labour.** In the south the typical extra user is a family member, so Core's two Team seats fit many farms. In staffed and northern operations the extra users are employees who turn over, and that's where Team's unlimited seats and fast induction earn their price.
- **New Zealand.** FarmIQ competes locally on NZFAP, NAIT and a free tier. The job matches the Australian one, with a different regulator and a stronger local competitor.

## 3. Value Proposition

Mobble already has a strategy to build on: three jobs on the homepage (records, compliance, team) and, from November 2026, a Team plan with unlimited members. This section turns that into one tagline, five sub-benefits and a promise for each role on and around the farm.

### What Mobble promises today

The homepage leads with a category and an attribute, "Simple Farm & Livestock Management Software", and puts the outcome in the subhead: "Keep livestock records, stay compliant, and coordinate your team in one powerful app designed for farmers worldwide."

Five themes recur across the site, pricing, newsletters and webinars: **simplicity**; **three jobs** (records, compliance, team); **farmer-built and farmer-supported** ("By farmers. For Farmers."); **access anywhere**, online and offline; and **fair, low-risk buying** (rolling-average pricing, free import, no double-paying when switching).

Where the promise falls short:
- **The outcome comes second.** A farmer arriving after a stressful audit or a staff mix-up has to read past the category to find their problem.
- **Nine equal feature cards**, each a noun, give basic cropping records the same weight as compliance and team.
- **Compliance is generic.** The homepage names no scheme, while customers talk about LPA accreditation, a JBS audit and NZFAP. It's silent on the LPA biosecurity-plan upload, mandatory from 1 September 2026.
- **Connect members get no promise of their own**, though Connect is the growth path.
- **Proof is dated and inconsistent.** "More than 3,000" (CTA), "3.8K+" (counter) and "more than 4,000" (newsletter) are live together, most named stories date from 2020 to 2023, and there are no outcome figures.
- **Pricing is out of sight.** The homepage shows no price and no mention of the Team plan's unlimited members.

### Narrowing to a tagline and five sub-benefits

> **One simple farm record your whole team keeps. Audit-ready every day.**

Supporting line: *Every mob, paddock, treatment and job on one map your family, staff and advisers share, on your phone or computer, even without signal.*

Why this tagline:
- **"Your whole team keeps"** names what customers praise most and what the Team plan monetises. A record the team keeps stays current, which makes the other benefits true.
- **"Audit-ready every day"** names the purchase trigger: an audit, LPA reaccreditation every two years, the 1 September 2026 biosecurity-plan rule.
- **"Simple"** keeps the brand promise, and the line travels across sheep, cattle and mixed farms, Australia and New Zealand (LPA and NZFAP), and North America ("ranch record").

The five sub-benefits:
1. **Audit-ready every day.** Treatments, batches, expiry dates and withholding periods are logged as the work happens; give your auditor 30-day read-only access or export the records. Name LPA and NZFAP on the page.
2. **Everyone on the same page, without the phone calls.** Family, staff and contractors share one live map, mobs, tasks and chat, so new starters find their way from day one.
3. **Know what you've got and where it is.** Live mob numbers and locations, with every move, birth, death and sale counted, so stocktake becomes a quick check.
4. **Tax time without the spreadsheet chase.** Your accountant, agent and adviser get their own login and see only what they need, including the financial-year livestock reconciliation. This doubles as the Connect promise.
5. **Works where your phone signal doesn't.** Record in the paddock or the yards offline; it syncs back in range.

What we cut, and why:
- **Grazing (B8)** is a vitamin for most farms, and Mobble has records without feed budgets or planning. AgriWebb and Atlas Grazing both sell planning, so leading with grazing invites a comparison Mobble loses. It becomes a feature section and a rotational-grazing page.
- **Easy switching (B9)** lowers the risk of buying without creating the desire to buy, so it sits beside the price.
- **Water and fences (B10)** need hardware most customers don't have yet. They go in the integrations strip.
- **New starters (B3) and treatments (B7)** are moments inside B2 and B1. Merging keeps the five distinct.
- **Support** is why customers believe the benefits, so it moves to the proof strip. Charlie Wootton, Jigsaw Farms VIC: "Jock and the team are very approachable and adaptable to our needs."
- **"More productive and profitable"** has no customer evidence behind a figure yet.

Scale belongs to AgriWebb, Halter and URUS; branding carries no premium; there's no cornered resource; process power is slow and hard to prove.

Other measures tell the same story:

Why it matters: the customer mix has shifted twice, so the first question is which segments drove each phase and whether the Team plan aims at the right one. Mobble also reports businesses, farmers, Mobblers and properties as headline numbers, and a growth target needs one agreed definition (paying accounts, active properties or weekly active users) first.

### The hypothesis in brief

Mobble grows today through a farmer-to-farmer engine: **word of mouth** (formalised in December 2025 as a referral programme), **a regional field team**, **co-marketing** with the agtech tools farmers already run, and frequent **first-year discounts**. In Mobble's words, "Our growth comes from farmers talking to other farmers." That engine produces the flat net adds above, which is slow for a company whose Head of Product brief is to "continue to scale across AU/NZ and beyond".

Our hypothesis is that the next stage of growth should run through people who touch many farms, and that the November 2026 Team plan is the hinge. Team gives a farm unlimited Team and Connect Members (accountants, advisers, agents and contractors), and each of those outsiders serves dozens of other farms.

Today that exposure mostly goes nowhere. There are about 1.4 users per property, partners have no multi-client view, nothing shows Mobble attributing sign-ups to a Connect member, and the referral programme is "for individuals (no ABNs/business referrals)", which shuts out the accountants and agents best placed to refer.

So the short-term job is to prove three channels by hand: **Team upgrades at renewal**, **stock agent and rural accountant sessions**, and **compliance-deadline clinics**. The long-term job is to build what closes them into loops: a multi-farm partner view, invite and referral attribution, and compliance tools that visitors, agents and auditors touch.

New segments need traction before loops. None of the 30 farms named on Mobble's site is in the NT, the Kimberley or North America, and the US and Canadian sites reuse AU and NZ testimonials.

### Long-term growth loops

In each loop the output becomes the next cycle's input.

#### Loop 1: Team and Connect invite (priority 1)

Roles: end users, Connect members, decision makers. The mechanism is in use: Billy Buckingham gives his nutritionist access, Henry Hindmarsh his accountant and contractors, and Natalie Engel shares the map with her chopper pilot. But the loop is open: exposure happens and no sign-ups come back that anyone measures.

- **Input:** a paying farm on Team.
- **Action:** the owner invites staff, contractors and Connect members "with access set to suit each role".
- **Output:** more records per farm (retention), and outsiders who ask their other farms to share access in Mobble. Those farms sign up and invite their own members.
- **Metrics:** active members per property (Team against Core); Connect members linked to two or more farms; trials attributed to an inviter; k = invites per account × acceptance rate × share who bring in another farm × trial-to-paid.
- **Product changes:** a multi-farm partner view with "add a client farm"; "who invited you?" at sign-up; contractor access that follows the contractor (it lasts 30 days today); a QR gate sign-in to the visitor log; invite prompts at mustering, EOFY and audits.

#### Loop 2: Farmer referral (priority 2)

Decision maker to decision maker. Mobble calls word of mouth its engine, but the referred share of new accounts isn't public.

- **Input:** an active farmer after a good season.
- **Action:** they share a link; the referee gets a trial and $100 off, the referrer $100 cash.

#### Loop 3: Adviser and accountant channel (priority 3)

Connect members are the channel and farmers the buyers. Xero is the analog: its Australian managing director said in 2013, "Seventy percent of our new customers come via the accountant or bookkeepers." Agworld "unites agronomists and their growers". Mobble's own antilog is a referral programme that excludes businesses. Status: a strong analog and no Mobble data.

- **Input:** a firm that serves many farms.

- **Output:** several client farms per firm and staff who recommend the product. Firms join because clients ask, and a partner directory sends farms to firms.
- **Metrics:** firms with three or more client farms; firm-sourced trials and conversion; reconciliation reports opened by accountants; churn of firm-linked farms against others.
- **Product changes:** a multi-client dashboard (withholding alerts, overdue tasks, "reconciliation ready"); partner tiers and certification; a client-invite flow; a public partner directory.

Working at small scale: Agworld supplied a 2026 customer story, and Farmo's founder sees Mobble as the farm's daily screen.

- **Input:** an integration with a partner's installed base (Agworld, Farmbot, Farmo, Farm Focus, then Optiweigh).
- **Action:** joint webinars, partner offers, partner-supplied stories, listings on the partner's site.
- **Output:** partner customers trial Mobble and get a daily reason to open it. Better retention and stories attract the next partner.
- **Metrics:** partner-sourced trials; attach rate; 12-month retention of integrated accounts; time to ship an integration.
- **Product changes:** a public API and partner documentation (neither exists); an eID and weighing partner (Optiweigh, or Datamars, which launched a new platform in September 2026).

Decision makers, with auditors and agents as amplifiers. Audits are the most common value story among named customers. As Tom Small put it, "When you're getting audited ... you can just show them the app". With no traffic data public, it's a hypothesis.

- **Metrics:** organic sessions to compliance pages, tool sign-ups, trials and conversion, by state.

## 6. Business Model

### The hypothesis in brief

Mobble is a self-serve annual subscription priced by livestock capacity: a rolling 12-month average, with 6 sheep counted as 1 cattle equivalent. There are two plans, Core and Team, and custom Enterprise rates above 50,000 sheep or 8,333 cattle. The owner pays; staff and outside partners don't. Revenue is almost all subscription. Prices here exclude GST.

Our hypothesis is that the economics rest on long customer lifetimes earned through high-touch support. The three biggest levers, in order, are **retention**, **cheaper acquisition through people who serve many farms** (Connect members) and **the Core-to-Team upgrade**, which matters more for retention than revenue.

We estimate annual recurring revenue (ARR) of about A$1.1M to A$2.7M, midpoint about A$1.7M. That fits a 14-person team funded mostly by its customers.

### User roles: who pays, who uses, who influences

| Role | Pays? | Influence on the model | Business lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Decision maker:** the Owner (the only one who can change or cancel the subscription) and Admins | Yes | Chooses the plan; the band follows herd size | Revenue per account, retention |
| **End users:** farm hands, family, contractors | No | Daily use keeps records current, which decides renewal. A third member pushes the farm to Team | Members per property, upgrades |
| **Connect members:** accountant, agent, adviser, auditor and contractor; agronomists through Agworld | No | Recommend Mobble or push for another tool. A third one triggers Team | Acquisition cost, upgrades |
| **Integration partners:** Agworld, Farmbot, Farmo, Farm Focus | No revenue share | Co-marketing, shared customers | Acquisition, retention |
| **Enterprise buyers** above 50,000 sheep equivalents | Custom rates | Ask for "prioritised feature requests" and migration help | Contract value, roadmap cost |

Why it matters: one person pays and many create the value. The model works when the owner can see the team's contribution is worth the Team premium, which is a product problem (showing who contributed what) as much as a pricing one.

- **Team** (marked RECOMMENDED): unlimited Team and Connect Members, up to 50,000 sheep or 8,333 cattle.

### The November 2026 shift to Team, and what it signals

The old tiers mixed herd size and team size, and Connect sat outside user limits. By November 2026 Team was the recommended plan, Connect was capped at two on Core, and the top band was Team-only. Mobble's reason: "The more your team contributes to Mobble, the easier it is to keep farm records up to date."

What it signals:

- **Two pricing axes:** herd size for business size, collaboration for how much the farm depends on Mobble.
- **No seat friction where collaboration happens**, which suits the invite loop.
- **Connect becomes an upgrade lever**, though the smallest farms, probably most accounts, can bring in only two outsiders.
- **The price level barely moved:** about A$32 to A$313 a month, close to the "$30 to $340+ per month" quoted in October 2024.
- **Open questions:** were existing accounts grandfathered, and what happened to Core accounts with more than two Connect members?

How much is the upgrade worth? If 70% of about 1,736 accounts are on Core and 30% of those upgrade at an average A$200 to A$300 premium, ARR rises A$73K to A$109K, about 4% to 6%. The Team plan's case rests on retention and acquisition.

- **One user, up to 4,000 sheep:** A$408 a year (2020), A$528 (2023), A$600 (2025–26), A$720 on Core (2026). About +76% in six years, roughly 9.6% a year, with +20% at the switch.
- **Team of 3–5, about 10,000 sheep:** A$1,440 to A$1,495, about +4%.
- **Team of 3–5, about 40,000 sheep:** A$3,120 to A$3,750, about +20%.
- **Up to 800 sheep:** A$360 to A$380 on Core (+6%) or A$440 on Team (+22%).

The rolling average softens these rises (a farm with a seasonal peak can drop a band), and Team removes the old surcharge for larger teams.

Why it matters: four pricing bases in six years, repeated rises, and users kept growing. That shows some pricing power, tempering the "no pricing power" reading in Competitive Advantage. The frequent discounts may be doing acquisition work rather than covering weak willingness to pay; only renewal data at each change can tell.

### Revenue sources

- **Subscriptions only.** No sign-up fee, add-ons, transaction or partner revenue.
- **Revenue given away:** first-year discounts of 15% to 50%, the $100 referral credit, switcher waivers, bushfire relief subscriptions.
- **Contrast:** AgriWebb sells setup packs from US$200 to US$4,000 and an A$300-a-year planning add-on.
- **Each future option carries a conflict.** Paid modules clash with "every Mobble feature"; paid partner seats slow the Connect loop; data products run into Mobble's promise not to share data without consent.

Our hypothesis: keep revenue simple and grow it through retention and acquisition.

### Lifetime value

**LTV = realised revenue per account (ARPA) × gross margin ÷ annual churn.** With expansion, divide by churn minus net expansion.

Mobble publishes none of these inputs, so we assume ARPA of A$750, A$1,000 or A$1,250 (the band mixes below, less discounts), churn of 25%, 15% or 8% a year, gross margin of 70%, 80% or 85%, and net expansion of 0% to 5%.

| Band | Small-skew mix | Base mix | Large-skew mix |
|---|---|---|---|
| < 800 | 45% | 35% | 25% |
| < 4,000 | 30% | 30% | 30% |
| < 12,000 | 15% | 20% | 25% |
| < 24,000 | 7% | 10% | 12% |
| < 50,000 (Team) | 2.5% | 4% | 6% |
| Enterprise (A$6,000 assumed) | 0.5% | 1% | 2% |
| List ARPA, all Core | about A$817 | A$979 | about A$1,170 |
| List ARPA, 20% on Team | A$847 | A$1,014 | A$1,211 |

The base mix implies about 6,440 sheep equivalents per account; Mobble's stock counts give about 6,400.

| Gross margin | Churn 8% (12.5-year life) | Churn 15% (6.7 years) | Churn 25% (4 years) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70% | A$8,750 | A$4,667 | A$2,800 |
| 80% | A$10,000 | A$5,333 | A$3,200 |
| 85% | A$10,625 | A$5,667 | A$3,400 |

LTV runs from **A$2,100** (750 × 0.70 ÷ 0.25) through **A$5,333** (1,000 × 0.80 ÷ 0.15) to **A$13,281** (1,250 × 0.85 ÷ 0.08), or **A$8,000** at mid with 5% expansion. Cutting churn from 15% to 10% lifts mid LTV by 50%, which is why retention ranks first.

At a 3 to 1 LTV-to-CAC target:

- Mobble can spend about A$700, A$1,778 or A$4,427 to win an account.
- A bottom-band account's ceiling is about A$405, so small farms need near-free channels.
- A referred account costs A$200 and pays back in about three months.
- A field day costing A$5,000 to A$15,000 needs about 3 to 9 paying accounts.

### Business equations by role

| Lever | Decision makers | End users | Connect members | Enterprise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue streams | Trial starts by channel × trial-to-paid | A third member triggers Team | Connect-sourced trials; a third member triggers Team | Opportunities × win rate |
| Payment model | Band price × (1 − first-year discount) | Team premium A$60 to A$480 | None | Custom, above A$3,750 |
| Lifetime value | Retention, band drift, upgrades | Hypothesis: retention rises with members | Hypothesis: firm-linked farms churn less | Net revenue retention |
| Cost structure | Field and support team; discounts, payouts | Support per member | Partner enablement; multi-farm view | Founder-led selling, migration, security |

- **Decision makers:** new ARR = Σ channels (trial starts × trial-to-paid) × list ARPA × (1 − discount). Next year = ARR × (1 − churn) + band drift + upgrades.
- **Team plan:** uplift = Core accounts × share at the 2 + 2 cap × upgrade rate × premium. For example, 1,215 × 30% × A$200 = A$73K.
- **Connect members:** new accounts = paying accounts × Connect members per account × share serving two or more farms × referrals per member × trial-to-paid. None of these is measured today, so the equation is a list of what to instrument.
- **Enterprise:** ARR = opportunities × win rate × contract value, carried by net revenue retention.
- **Partners:** ARR = partner-sourced trials × trial-to-paid × ARPA, plus retention gains.
- **The whole:** ARR next year = ARR × (1 − churn + expansion) + self-serve + Connect + partner + enterprise.

### Sensitivities

At the midpoint, each point of churn is worth about A$17K of ARR a year, and moving churn from 15% to 12% raises LTV by 25%. A 30% Core-to-Team upgrade adds 4% to 6% to ARR. Halving the first-year discount adds A$150 per new account in year one.

## 7. Cohesion and Conflict Assessment

A check across the six dimensions: does each hypothesis support the others, and does anything claimed in one go missing or get contradicted in another?

### Where the dimensions hold together

| Thread | Problem | Audience | Value proposition | Competitive advantage | Growth | Business model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Proof on demand** | Top painkiller: proving compliance when asked | Strongest in LPA- and NZFAP-audited Now segments | "Audit-ready every day" | Part of the short-term win in every Now segment | Compliance-deadline clinics and content loop | Triggers trials at fixed dates |
| **One record the whole team keeps** | Records made in the paddock; owner as bottleneck | Multi-property family businesses with staff | "Everyone on the same page" and the tagline | Switching costs grow with people and history | Team upgrade at the pre-renewal call | Team plan prices collaboration |
| **People who serve many farms** | Accountant, auditor and agronomist jobs | Connect members as a separate audience | "Tax time without the spreadsheet chase" | Network economies (to build) | Invite loop; adviser and accountant loop | Connect-sourced acquisition equation |
| **Simplicity** | Tools "too clunky, over-complicated" at founding | Prefers simple over deep | "Simple" kept in the tagline | Mob-first and fluent in sheep | Farmer word of mouth | All features on every plan; price set by stock and team size only |

The skeleton is sound. Every sub-benefit maps to a ranked problem, both primary powers sit behind named sub-benefits, and the business equations price the two behaviours the moat depends on: more people and more history.

### Conflicts and tensions

**C1. The moat depends on Connect, but current settings hold it back.** Connect is the power to build and its invite loop ranks first in Growth Strategy. Yet Core caps Connect at two members (the help centre still says they're free), referrals are for individuals only, and partners get no multi-client view.
- **Why it matters:** the cheapest acquisition channel in this narrative is the one product and pricing restrict.
- **Resolution:** treat Connect as distribution, separate from seat packaging: free Connect on every plan, business referrals for partners, a multi-farm partner view.
- **Settles it:** the Connect graph (Q4) and Connect-role interviews (R4).

**C2. "Audit-ready" promises more than the product holds.** Mobble names only LPA and NZFAP, with no eNVD, NLIS transfer, PIC or NAIT link, biosecurity-plan template or visitor register. AgriWebb offers eNVD and NLIS submission.
- **Why it matters:** the newest triggers (the biosecurity plan from 1 September 2026, eID from 1 January 2027) sit just outside scope.
- **Resolution:** scope the promise to the records Mobble holds, then test a thin compliance bridge: a biosecurity-plan file slot, visitor log, PIC fields and eNVD hand-off.
- **Settles it:** R1 and R2, plus counts of compliance files already uploaded.

**C3. Simplicity versus the eID cliff.** The Head of Product role asks for "the tough calls on what not to build, protecting the simple, easy-to-use experience". But the Now audience is sheep-heavy, and from 1 January 2027 every sheep and farmed goat leaving a property in NSW, Queensland, SA, Tasmania and the ACT needs an eID. Mobble's help centre says "Excel will be your best friend".
- **Why it matters:** this is the single assumption (R1) that decides what Mobble is.
- **Resolution:** measure the hidden demand first (Q5), then choose between a bridge to partners' eID systems and staying out.
- **Settles it:** the R1 thresholds.

**C4. The Now audience is sheep-led, but the stock base leans to cattle.** Beef cow-calf families rank fifth, yet cattle are about 55% of Mobble's stocking units. Against AgriWebb's DSE values, we estimate the 6:1 ratio charges cattle operations about 12.5% more per unit of grazing than sheep operations.
- **Why it matters:** the segment bringing the most revenue by stocking unit may be the least served in features and messaging.
- **Resolution:** keep sheep and mixed families as the Now wedge, move cattle features (live weights through Optiweigh, cattle-specific compliance) up the roadmap, and test cattle messaging.
- **Settles it:** revenue and retention by species mix, which Mobble already holds.

**C5. Small holders: high pain, low price tolerance, high support cost.** Small holdings face the same audits but pay about A$19 a head a year at 20 cattle. Free tiers (FarmIQ Grow, Herdwatch) anchor the price at zero, and Mobble's model is high-touch.
- **Why it matters:** clinics aimed at small holders could add accounts that cost more to support than they pay.
- **Resolution:** keep small holders in Future with a low-touch, compliance-led offer: self-serve onboarding and templated audit packs, with no extra people.
- **Settles it:** R6 and support tickets per account by band.

**C6. Company direction versus evidence on North America.** The Regional Success Lead posting speaks of expanding "into the Americas", but no North American customer is named and the US and Canadian sites reuse AU and NZ testimonials.
- **Why it matters:** spreading a 14-person team across three regions slows the AU and NZ loops this narrative depends on.
- **Resolution:** one reference ranch and local compliance content before spend, plus co-selling with Ranchbot.
- **Settles it:** R5 and North American trial-to-paid conversion (Q1).

**C7. Discounting versus brand and willingness to pay.** Frequent first-year discounts (20% EOFY 2025, 50% birthday 2025, 15% October 2026, 30% November 2026) leave the brand with no pricing power yet. But list price for a one-user farm of up to 4,000 sheep rose about 76%, from A$408 a year (August 2020) to A$720 (November 2026), while users kept growing. The discounts may be buying acquisition while willingness to pay holds up.
- **Why it matters:** constant discounts train buyers to wait and blur the willingness-to-pay signal the new Team premium needs.
- **Resolution:** tie offers to compliance moments and switchers in place of the calendar.
- **Settles it:** retention and expansion by acquisition offer (Q1, Q3).

**C8. Grazing: cut from the value proposition, kept on the roadmap.** Value Proposition treats grazing as a vitamin, Mobble frames its grazing tools for "more intensive grazing", and Halter and Atlas Grazing now sell planning.
- **Why it matters:** building planning means competing head-on with funded specialists for a vitamin.
- **Resolution:** keep grazing as a segment page and a records feature, and integrate (Halter, Farmo) for planning.
- **Settles it:** R8 card rankings by segment.

**C9. The evidence base has a selection bias.** Most customer evidence comes from Mobble's own case studies: 13 of the 30 named farms are in Victoria, several quotes date from 2021 to 2023, and only two stories were published in 2025. Help articles also contradict shipped features (backdating 14 versus 60 days; "no print" versus map printing shipped in October 2025).
- **Why it matters:** some High confidence ratings rest on marketing material, and stale help undercuts "simple" just when a user needs help.
- **Resolution:** sample churned and lost-trial farmers as well as advocates, and treat help-centre accuracy as product quality.
- **Settles it:** interview wave 1.

### What this means for a Head of Product's first quarter

The conflicts cluster into five decisions, in order of impact:

1. **Decide Connect's role (C1):** a seat inside pricing, or a distribution channel? Instrument the Connect graph either way.
2. **Size the eID question with data before debating it (C3):** run Q5 and the R1 interviews.
3. **Scope and extend "audit-ready" (C2):** a thin compliance bridge, biosecurity plan first.
4. **Rebalance toward cattle, where the stocking units already are (C4),** without the pricing ratio penalising them.
5. **Gate new regions (C6):** one reference customer and local compliance content before spend.

All five serve the role's two explicit asks: protect simplicity, and speed up the development team by agreeing on what not to build.

## 8. Narrative Validation Plan

The six dimensions are hypotheses. This section sets out how to find the wrong ones before engineering time goes into them: what other companies' wins and retreats tell us, a two-wave interview plan, the riskiest assumptions with kill criteria, and five checks Mobble can run on its own data.

5. **Properties:** how many, and how far apart the furthest two are.
6. **People who record or look up livestock information:** 1, 2–3, 4–10, 11+.
7. **Current records** (paper, spreadsheet, Mobble, AgriWebb, Atlas Grazing or MaiaGrazing, Halter, FarmIQ, Herdwatch, Breedr, Stockbook or Stockhand, Agworld, other) and for how long.
8. **Lapsed apps:** any farm records app dropped in the last two years, and why (fills segment D).
9. **Audits:** LPA, processor (for example JBS), NZFAP, organic, other or none; when the last was, and whether it was scheduled or random.
10. **eID:** whether they record individual animals, and for which stock.
11. **Outside eyes:** who sees their livestock numbers (accountant, agent, consultant, bank, none), and how they're sent.
12. **Coverage** across the paddocks: none, patchy or good.
13. **Conflict check:** anyone in the household working in farm software or agtech ends the screener.
14. **Format:** phone or video, best times, months to avoid.

Connect-role screener:

- Role and firm type.
- Number of farm clients, and the share running livestock.
- Software used with farm clients (Xero, Figured, Agworld, AgriWebb, Mobble, other).
- How, and how often, they get clients' livestock numbers.
- How many clients have given them access to Mobble or another farm app.
- Phone or video, and best times.

**Quotas:** no more than two interviews per family business; at least two sheep-led and two cattle-led properties in each farmer segment; at least a third of segments A, F and G with patchy or no coverage.

#### Incentives and timing

- **Farmers (A to F, H), 45 minutes:** A$100 e-gift card or the same amount to a local cause they pick, with account credit as only one of three choices so customers aren't nudged toward praise. Academic rates of $40 to $50 undershoot what a business owner gives up.
- **Others:** northern station managers and North American ranchers A$150 (or US$100); Connect professionals and auditors A$150, since they bill by the hour; staff and family (J) A$50 for 20 minutes. If fewer than 7 in 10 of the first ten bookings show, raise the farmer rate to A$150.
- **Wave 1, 9 November to 11 December 2026 (30 interviews):** customers (A, B), churned and lost trials (D), staff (J) and Connect roles (K to N), testing problem, value proposition and business model. It overlaps southern harvest and shearing, so favour evening phone slots and hold mixed croppers (H) for wave 2.
- **Wave 2, 1 February to 19 March 2027 (26 interviews):** northern stations (G, late wet season), North America (I, before spring calving), AgriWebb users (E), paper users (F), small holders (C), mixed farms (H) and experts (O), testing audience, competitive advantage and growth for the Future segments.
- **Scheduling:** phone by default, since 63% of NSW Farmers respondents report dropouts. Offer early-morning and 7:30 to 9 pm slots and an SMS reminder the day before. We assume the windows to avoid are southern lambing, marking and shearing (timing varies by district), grain harvest (roughly October to January), the northern wet and first muster, and NZ lambing and calving (roughly August to October). Q5 checks these against Mobble's own activity.

#### The 45-minute interview guide

A lead asks and a note-taker logs quotes with timestamps; rotate the lead across the Head of Product, customer success and an engineer. Don't demo, correct or sell. If time runs short, the riskiest assumptions come first.

- **0:00–0:03 Open.** Consent to record, and "we're here to learn, and nothing here is a sales call".
- **0:03–0:08 Context.** Enterprises, stock, properties and people. Who writes records down, and where?
- **0:08–0:16 Problem.** The last time a record couldn't be found quickly. The last audit, from notice to the auditor leaving: preparation time, and what the auditor asked to see. The last time a mob count or location was wrong. How staff learn what needs doing today.
  - **Problem card sort:** ten cards (audit preparation; stock numbers and locations; withholding mistakes; telling staff what to do; orienting new staff; tax-time reconciliation; grazing decisions; water and pump failures; eID or individual records; stock owned by someone else). Sort into "costs me money or risk this year", "annoying" and "doesn't matter", rank the first pile, and say which they've already paid to solve.
- **0:16–0:20 Audience and roles.** Who decided how records are kept, who'd have to agree to a change, and who could block it? What changed when they last looked for a tool? Small holders: shared paddocks or co-owned stock, and how records and treatment costs are split.
- **0:20–0:28 Benefit stack rank.** Ten benefit cards (below) in random order: top three, bottom three, then a full rank. For the top card: "Why that one? Would you tell a neighbour?" Then show Mobble's homepage headline: "In your own words, what does this do, and what would it cost?" and "What would your top line say?"
- **0:28–0:33 Competitive advantage.** What else they looked at, what tipped it, and what would make them switch. AgriWebb users: what's changed since the URUS deal in May? Awareness of Halter, Breedr, Atlas Grazing and FarmIQ. Sheep producers in NSW, QLD, SA, TAS or the ACT: the eID plan for 1 January 2027, and where those records will live. Customers: "How would you feel if you could no longer use Mobble?" (the Sean Ellis question).
- **0:33–0:38 Growth.** How they heard and who they asked; whether they've recommended it; whether an accountant, agent or adviser has suggested software; whether they'd invite them into their records.
- **0:38–0:44 Willingness to pay.** Van Westendorp's four questions in A$ a year ex GST for their band: too expensive to consider, so cheap they'd doubt it, starting to feel expensive, a bargain. Then show Core and Team for their band (under 4,000 sheep: Core A$720, Team A$880) with the member limits (2 Team and 2 Connect members against unlimited). Which would they pick, what would make Team worth the difference, who pays from which budget, and monthly or yearly?
- **0:44–0:45 Close.** "What should we have asked?" Ask for one or two introductions (a neighbour, agent or accountant).

| 3 | Everyone (family, staff, contractors) works from the same map and records, with fewer phone calls |

**Role variants**
- **Staff and family (J), 20 minutes:** context, staff problems and the stack rank. Swap pricing for "What makes you skip entering something? When did the app let you down?"
- **Connect roles (K to N), 30 to 45 minutes:** how they get client livestock data now and what's usually wrong with it; a six-card ranking (no more chasing spreadsheets; standard-format reconciliation; all client farms in one list; tasks for the farmer; time-limited read-only access; data into my own software); whether they'd ask clients to use one system, and whether they'd pay, or expect to be paid, for a practice view.
- **Northern stations (G):** add water runs and bores, staff turnover and seasonal onboarding, time out of coverage, AE and LSU units, and which records Halter or others already keep.
- **North America (I):** local words (herds, pastures, ranch); records that buyers, lenders or state programmes require; calving records by animal.

#### Debrief template

One per interview within 24 hours, in the same structure so synthesis can count across interviews:

- **Header:** ID, date, interviewers, segment, role, region, enterprise mix, capacity band, people who touch records, tools, coverage, audits and last audit.
- **Context** in two sentences.
- **Problems:** top three cards, which they've paid to solve, and a 1 to 5 painkiller score with evidence.
- **Trigger** for looking for a tool, or why they never did.
- **Benefits:** top and bottom three cards, and why number one, in their words.
- **Their homepage line**, in their words.
- **Alternatives**, switching reasons and barriers.
- **Sean Ellis answer** and why (customers only).
- **Growth:** how they heard, who they told, whether they'd invite their agent or accountant.
- **Willingness to pay:** four prices, Core or Team and why, who pays.
- **Best three quotes**, with timestamps, and any **surprises** that contradict the narrative.
- **Scorecard:** supports, contradicts or neutral for each dimension, and confirm, kill or unclear for each of R1 to R8 touched.
- **Follow-ups** and introductions offered.

#### Synthesis method

- **Code** every debrief against one tag set (dimensions, R1 to R8, segment, role, enterprise) in a shared repository; a spreadsheet works.
- **Score the rankings** as Borda scores (rank 1 of 10 earns 10 points), by segment and role.
- **Plot a painkiller matrix:** for each problem, how many put it in "costs me money or risk" against how many have paid to solve it. The top right is the value proposition.
- **Report willingness to pay as ranges** per band; 3 to 6 people per segment is too few for curves. If the ranges flag a problem, run the questions as an in-app survey.
- **Score each segment** 1 to 5 on problem intensity, product fit, willingness to pay and reachability to set the Now and Future order.
- **Read out and rewrite** with the CEO, Head of Engineering and customer success. After wave 1, update Problem, Value Proposition and Business Model; after wave 2, Target Audience, Competitive Advantage and Growth Strategy. Then re-run the cohesion check, flagging any top-three benefit missing from the homepage and any claimed advantage nobody ranks.
